Home / Blog / Popcorn Typing tips

June 20, 2026 · updated August 11, 2026 Guide

There is a note in my own config file saying the machines are useless

I made this game. What follows is taken out of my own balance file, comments included.

Popcorn Typing has frying pans, microwaves, and eventually machinery of increasingly silly scale. It looks exactly like every idle game you have played, so you do what idle games have trained you to do: you buy the machines.

Buried in the balance file, in a comment nobody was ever supposed to read, is this instruction to myself:

Manual input is strongest, so keep the machines' output low. Their job is to look good sitting on the screen popping away.

The equipment in this game is scenery. I built it that way deliberately, and then never told anybody.

What actually produces popcorn

One line governs the entire game:

kernels per keystroke = variety × level × combo

Three multipliers, all of which you drive with your hands. The machines are not in that equation at all. They add a trickle on top.

The combo is where the game is won

Consecutive correct keystrokesMultiplier
0x1
10x2
25x3
50x4
100x6
200x10

One mistake sends this to zero. Not down a step. Zero.

Two hundred consecutive correct keystrokes for the top multiplier is the entire skill ceiling of the game, and it is brutal in a way I did not fully appreciate until I tried to do it myself. Somewhere past a hundred you start noticing your own fingers, which is exactly when you fumble.

The practical consequence is unintuitive: slow down. A steady rhythm at x10 beats frantic typing that keeps resetting to x1. Accuracy is not a nice-to-have here, it is the multiplier.

Varieties are the big jumps

VarietyKernels per characterCost
Ordinary corn1free, you start here
Caramel corn152,500
Truffle salt corn350250,000
Solid gold corn9,00020,000,000

Fifteen times, then twenty-three times, then twenty-six times. These are not incremental upgrades, they are the spine of your progression, and they are the thing to save for.

Gold corn does not stop. After you own it, each further research level doubles your per-character output, with the cost rising 2.1x each time. That is the endgame: there is no final variety, just an ever-doubling one.

Level is the cheapest multiplier in the game

Level 2 costs 60 kernels. Sixty. Each level after that costs 1.5x the last, which stays affordable far longer than anything else on offer.

Level multiplies your output directly, and it also increases how many kernels visibly burst per keystroke, one extra per level, capped at 18 for the sake of the poor browser.

If you take one thing from this page: early on, buy levels before machines. Level 2 costs 60. The first frying pan costs 60 and produces 1.5 kernels per second. The level is not close to a fair fight.

Finish the word

Completing a whole word pays a bonus of characters x base kernels x 5, plus a large burst. Abandoning a word halfway through pays nothing at all.

So where do the machines fit

They earn while you are not typing, and that is genuinely all. Cost growth is 1.18 per unit bought, and the price bands sit about ten times apart so there is always a visible next goal.

Buy them when you have spare kernels, not instead of levels and varieties. And enjoy them, because honestly the popping animation is most of why they exist.

The containers are the actual story

Your goal is filling a container. Fill it, get a reward, move to a bigger one. There are sixteen, and the list is the most fun thing in the file.

The background switches to space partway up that list, around Earth. And filling the universe is not the end either, because past the last entry the requirement simply multiplies by eight forever.

I remember writing that line and thinking it was a cheap joke. It is the part of the game I am now fondest of.

Nobody has ever asked me any of this

The honest context for this page: not one person has ever sent me a question about this game. No comments, no messages, nothing. I have no idea whether anyone has hit x10 combo or reached the universe.

So this guide is not answering demand. It is me opening my own balance file, finding a note I left for myself about the machines being decoration, and realising that a player would have absolutely no way of knowing that.

If you do get to the universe, I would genuinely like to hear about it.

Popcorn Typing is free in the browser. It is Japanese typing practice, typed in romaji.
