Who Is Ame? A Village in the Shade Theory: A Harvest God and a Lion Dance

Clues from the tutorial to the ending, a shooting star, two stones, and a lion played by two.

Bubblegum · After finishing the game, both endings · September 24, 2026

Who is Ame, the protagonist of Village in the Shade?

Ame isn't just a child. There's evidence all over the village that Ame is a god, or a spirit. But what kind of god, where Ame came from, and why Ame was farming in this village, the game never says outright.

Spoilers up to the ending, including both endings. Best read after you finish. Ame is the default name in the Japanese version. The god behind the village head's house is called ヅ主 (Zunushi) in Japanese. I haven't been able to check its English name, so I use Zunushi here.

The story up to the ending

Ame comes down from the mountains with no memory and starts farming in the village of Kagatsu. But the village has a ritual that offers a human sacrifice every ten years. Twenty years ago, Kodama jumped into the great pit in place of Kisuke, who was supposed to be the sacrifice, and the body Ame is using is Kodama's. After fighting Kodama, who resents Ame, and then the land god, Ame faces one last choice on a white wasteland under the stars.

The clues that Ame is a god are scattered around the village from as early as the tutorial.

Kisuke always paid up front

Kisuke says he knew what Ame was right away, and explains the rule: a god who accepts something and is asked for a wish has to grant it.

That's why Kisuke always hands over money or tools first whenever there's a job to ask. I think the early farm work was Kisuke testing whether Ame really was a god. When Rin, the village head, orders Ame to cut down Kodama, Rin hands over a wallet first, too.

Kodama uses the same rule. Near the end, because of Kodama's wish, Ame is made to cut down Tobari, a friend.

Kodama gave a body, Kisuke gave an eye

Looking back on the day of the ritual twenty years ago, Kisuke says he prayed to a god, and the great rock in front of him seemed to glow faintly. That was all.

Ame is the one who accepted the body Kodama offered at the bottom of the pit. I think the rock glowed in front of Kisuke at that exact moment.

Kisuke gave Ame his left eye, too. He says he didn't give it to the god that threatens their lives, but chose to give it to the one who walked alongside them trying to make life better. Like Kodama giving a body, the act of offering is itself an admission that Ame is a god.

Zunushi calls Ame "my other half"

Zunushi calls Ame 我が片身, "my other half." The word means one half of a single body split in two. If Ame is Zunushi's other half, Ame is the same kind of god as Zunushi.

What were they before they split?

The gate to your farm is a torii

The gate you walk through every day to get to your farm isn't painted red, so it just looks like a gate. But look closely. It's the same shape as a torii, the gate at the entrance to a shrine.

The will the previous village head left for Rin says that if the child taken by the god ever comes back, let them live in that yard. The run-down yard Ame is given at the start was a place for a god to live from the very beginning, with a torii standing at its entrance.

The shooting star on the save screen

Every time you start the game, a shooting star falls across the save file screen, the one at the top of this post. It looks like decoration, but I think that shooting star is Ame.

A burning god fell from the sky

One of the Lost Books says that a burning god fell from the heavens, split in two, and was worshipped in the village. That's how a meteorite falls. It also explains why Zunushi calls Ame "my other half."

Both of the things they worship are stones

So where did the two halves of the god go?

Zunushi is worshipped in the great rock behind the village head's house.

What the villagers have worshipped at the crossroads shrine since long ago is a stone, too. After Ame rebuilds the shrine, someone asks who it's for, and one of the two answers is "myself." Do you remember that? It's so early in the game that I let it go by, but looking back, it was setting up the ending. It's not a throwaway joke option. Ame really is a god, and "myself" is the honest answer.

If the god split into two, there are two stones. The great rock is Zunushi, and the stone in the crossroads shrine is Ame.

When Ame dies, Ame falls under the stars

When Ame dies, Ame ends up on a white wasteland under a sky full of stars. It looked familiar, and then I realized it's the save screen.

  • The save screen: a shooting star falls.
  • The book: a burning god fell from the sky and split in two.
  • Zunushi: calls Ame "my other half."
  • The shrines: the great rock for Zunushi, the crossroads stone. Both stones.
  • After death: the same starry wasteland as the save screen.

Then the shooting star on the save screen is Ame, the god who fell on the village and was worshipped as a stone.

Ame might be a shishimai lion

So what does a stone god that fell from the sky do in this village? I think the closest thing is the lion of shishimai, the Japanese lion dance.

A clump of black fur, big swirling eyes, a mouth full of teeth. Zunushi's face looks just like a shishimai lion's head.

The shishimai lion isn't really a harvest god. It's a sacred beast that drives off evil spirits and purifies a place. Still, it has been danced at festivals praying for good harvests for a long time, and in dengaku, the rice-planting festival dances, the lion sometimes dances first. Some of the three-lion dances in the Kanto region are said to have started as dances to pray for rain. In the yamabushi kagura of the Tohoku region, the lion's head itself is called Gongen-sama and worshipped as a god.

Two people make one lion

Some shishimai have two people play one lion. The one in front holds the head, the one behind gets inside the body, and only together are they one lion.

Remember Zunushi calling Ame "my other half." If Zunushi is the lion's head, Ame is the other half. Maybe the god that fell from the sky and split in two was always a lion played by two.

A gongen is a Buddha who appears temporarily in the form of a god to save people. Zunushi tells Ame to save people, and Ame is in the village in Kodama's borrowed body. That every player's Ame looks different, thanks to character creation, makes sense too, if it's a temporary form.

The three sacred items are lion dance tools

The three sacred items you gather to defeat the land god line up with the tools of a lion dance.

Sacred itemHow you get itIn a lion dance
A nue's hideSumire entrusts it to youThe cloth that becomes the lion's body
A rock-cutting hatchetSazanka entrusts it to youThe blade that slays the lion
A whistleLearned from the song of Yuta's parrotThe flute in the music

In Kaga shishi, a lion dance from Ishikawa Prefecture, the lion's body is a huge mosquito net, and the flute and drum players all go inside it. At the end, a dancer with a sword or a naginata slays the lion. Slaying the lion drives away evil spirits, stamping the ground purifies it, and the whole performance prays for a good harvest.

The hide that becomes the lion's body goes into the hands of Ame, the lion's other half, which fits nicely. With the hide, the blade and the flute together, Ame goes to clear the land god's curse. It's the same order as Kaga shishi.

The last tools are meteoric iron

You upgrade your farm tools from copper to steel to gold, and the last step is meteoric iron. In farming games, the usual step after gold is a made-up metal like mithril, so meteoric iron is an unusual choice.

But if Ame is a stone god that fell from the sky, meteoric iron is iron from the same kind of rock as Ame. The fully upgraded hoe and watering can become sacred tools. That feels right to me.

In that book, too, what the villagers held up to call down the god of heaven was vegetables and fruit. It's a god called down to bless the harvest. Ame, the default name, can be read as 天, "heaven," or as 雨, "rain," which no field can do without. Since some lions danced to pray for rain, the rain reading might not be far off either.

Where it came from: a meteorite that fell burning from the sky and split in two. What it looks like: Zunushi is the lion's head and Ame its other half, one lion between two. Its tools: the three sacred items are a lion dance's hide, blade and flute, and the farm tools end in meteoric iron. Its job: clear curses, purify the soil, and make the fields grow.

So Ame is a harvest god who, together with Zunushi, makes one lion.

Not you as a god

Before the final fight, Kisuke says that if he asked Ame to save them, Ame would just be bound again. He's tired of binding and being bound. So he wants to say this not to Ame as a god, but to the Ame who lived here.

Kisuke, who always paid up front for every favor, doesn't make it a wish this one time.

What Kodama wished for at the bottom of the pit twenty years ago was to live in this village with everyone. Choose to go home together, and Ame turns Kodama into a peach sapling and plants it behind the farm.

Not in human form, but Kodama comes home to the village and grows beside Ame's farm. It's the kind of wish a harvest god would grant. Choose to stay, and Ame gives the body back to Kodama and disappears. Either way, what Ame protects in the end is life in this village.

The sacrifices that went on every ten years end here too. The man from the village head's family who said "I did my duty well" on the red phone, sometime in the 1990s, was one of them. I wrote about him in my red phone theory.

Who is Ame?

One half of a meteorite that fell burning from the sky and split in two. A harvest god who makes one lion with Zunushi, clears curses and makes the fields grow. That's how I read Ame.

But the one Kisuke talks to at the end isn't Ame the god. It's the Ame who farmed and lived in this village.

Next time you pick your save file, watch the star falling across the screen for a moment. It might be Ame, on the way down to the village.

What kind of god did the Ame on your farm look like? There are still clues nobody has explained, like Yoh's doodles and who Konno really is. Extra content has been announced too, so I'm looking forward to what comes next.
