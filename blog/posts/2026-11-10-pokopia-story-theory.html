Pokémon Pokopia · Story theory

Where Is Pokopia Set? A Story Theory (Spoilers)

Under the slow-life surface there's another story. The towns, a note left by a trainer and one gym leader's name all point the same way.

Bubblegum · Written from my own playthrough · November 12, 2025, updated after launch

This page talks about the story, including things you learn in the first two areas. If you want to know whether the game is for you, my spoiler-free review is the place to start.

When the first Pokémon Center is finally rebuilt, there's a record inside from a trainer who came here long ago. They had just left their partner in the Pokémon Center's PC. They had to evacuate soon, they wrote, but before that they wanted to go back to every town they had ever visited with that partner, one more time. They couldn't quite explain why.

Pokopia sells itself as a slow life with Pokémon, and at first it is a gentle town-building game. Play for a while, though, and you notice there are no humans anywhere. What's left is the shell of a Pokémon Center and the ruins of buildings. As I kept going, a hunch turned into something I was sure of: this is the Kanto region of the main games, a long time in the future, and a single Ditto is rebuilding it from nothing.

Here's the evidence, piece by piece.

Why the hero is a Ditto

Early on, the game makes it clear that your Ditto once had a trainer, and that the human shape it takes is a copy of that trainer. There's a Ditto sticker on the Pokédex, which tells you how much it was loved.

One thought made me laugh out loud while playing: maybe this is one of those perfect-stat Ditto we all left at the Day Care for breeding. If you've played Pokémon, you've probably done it. Where did all those Ditto end up? It's a strangely real feeling.

Read the trainer's record again with that in mind. The hero is the partner that trainer left in a PC box when they evacuated, and the game begins when that Ditto wakes up and comes out. It keeps the shape of a human because it's copying the trainer it misses. It isn't a Pokémon waiting for orders. It's a Ditto that stays in its trainer's form because it misses them, and I think that's what the game is really about.

Then there's the word "evacuate". Something forced people to leave, and it looks like the trainer never came back. That seems to be the reason the world is in ruins.

The first area is Fuchsia City

Where the first area, the Withered Wastelands, sits on the map was argued about before launch. While playing, I checked the two other popular answers first.

Not Cinnabar Island. Cinnabar is a volcanic island with sea all around it. The Withered Wastelands has the Cycling Road right next to it, and the road tiles at its edges don't match. There's no Cycling Road on Cinnabar.

Not Pallet Town. The online-only side of the game already has a place called Palette Town. The Pallet Town slot has been given to the Cloud Island side, so the first area isn't it.

Once the Pokémon Center is rebuilt, look around and you'll find road tiles you've seen before. They match the Fuchsia City road tiles from Pokémon: Let's Go, Pikachu! and Let's Go, Eevee! exactly, and the number of ruined houses next to them matches Fuchsia City too. Head north from the Pokémon Center and there's a fountain where the road to the Safari Zone used to be. Compared with the Fuchsia City map in HeartGold and SoulSilver, the water is in roughly the same place.

Fuchsia City has a Poison-type gym, run by Koga. Head east from the Pokémon Center and you find mentions of Koga, and of Janine, the gym leader in Gold and Silver. I'll come back to her.

The next area is Vermilion City

Go through the gate east of the Withered Wastelands and follow Route 15 to Route 13. It's hard to tell because everything is in ruins, but beyond that is the sea and an area called Bleak Beach. Keep going and you reach a Pokémon Center.

The moment I got there it clicked. North from Fuchsia, then out to the east, the sea in front of me, the position of the Pokémon Center. I thought, this is Vermilion City, and the feeling kept getting stronger.

The gym ruins are where the gym was

Starting from the Pokémon Center, there's a road north toward Saffron City and the ruins of a gym to the east. That's the same layout as Vermilion City in the original games. There are also traces of a building at almost the same direction and distance as the Pokémon Fan Club.

The S.S. Anne settles it

Cross the pier south of the gym ruins and there's a silhouette you'll recognise: a ship, and a windmill next to it. It's the S.S. Anne, Vermilion City's landmark ever since the first games.

The road tiles, the Pokémon Center, the direction of the gym ruins, the road to Saffron, the pier to the south and the S.S. Anne. The only place where all of them line up at once, connected to Fuchsia City, is Vermilion City. So this is Kanto. A Kanto in complete ruin, with no humans anywhere in it.

It happens after Gold and Silver

If it's Kanto, when is it? The game answers that too.

Among the notes about Fuchsia City's gym, the name Janine comes up as gym leader. Janine is Koga's daughter, and she ran the Fuchsia gym in Gold and Silver. In Red and Blue, it was Koga. If Janine is mentioned, then this world comes after Gold and Silver at the earliest.

The rebuilding starts in Fuchsia, the city of poison, in a time after the first games. I don't know what else to call that but irony. Details like this are why I don't see Pokopia as just a spin-off.

Where did the humans go?

"This is Kanto in ruins. So where did all the people go?" Anyone who plays it will probably wonder the same thing.

The game doesn't say, at least so far. There are hints. First, the word "evacuate" in the trainer's record. Then, after reading it, there were things in the world that made me think: so it was fire. The way buildings have collapsed, and what look like scorch marks, suggest a fire or some large event that involved one.

The Pokémon have changed too, which says a lot of time has passed. Peakychu, the pale Pikachu, looks faded, almost ghostly, and Mosslax is a Snorlax covered in moss. With no human hands to look after them, they changed over a very long time.

Why did people leave? The game seems to be holding that answer back on purpose. You get glimpses as the story goes on, and that's what keeps Pokopia from being only a cozy slow-life game.

What I got right before launch, and what I got wrong

Around November and December 2025, I wrote a few theories based on the trailers and early hands-on reports. Now that I've played it, here's how they did.

Right: a world without humans. A broken Pokédex in the trailer, no humans anywhere, and the weight of the phrase "from scratch" made me guess at a post-apocalyptic setting. The game confirmed it. At the time I half thought it couldn't really be that.

Right: Kanto, starting in Fuchsia. Fans overseas were suggesting Fuchsia City before launch. The matching tiles and Vermilion City settled it.

Right, but sadder than I thought: why the hero is a Ditto. I guessed it was there to make copying Pokémon moves make sense. The real reason goes further. It's a Ditto that keeps its trainer's shape because it misses them.

Wrong: Ditto as the villain. My contrarian theory was that Ditto was building a trap to lure humans in. It's the opposite. Ditto lost the trainer it loved and is rebuilding a ruined world in their shape. Completely wrong, and I'm glad.

Wrong: copying legendary Pokémon to remake the world. The building stays at about the level of Dragon Quest Builders. There's no sci-fi horror twist where you copy Arceus or Kyogre and reshape whole islands. That idea was a bit much.

Little by little, back to every town

The towns, the S.S. Anne and Janine's name all say the same thing. Pokopia is set in a Kanto from the far future, and the one walking through it is a Ditto still wearing the shape of the trainer it misses. Rebuilding the towns poco a poco, little by little, isn't really a slow-life story. It's a story about getting back what was lost.

The trainer wanted to see every town they'd visited with their partner one more time before they left. Now the partner is the one going back to those towns, one at a time, and putting them back together.

Why did people evacuate, and what did the fire leave behind? This world still has history it hasn't told. The further you play, the more of it you see.
