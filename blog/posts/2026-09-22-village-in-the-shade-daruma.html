Village in the Shade: How to Beat the Daruma (Find the Odd Stone)

A Red Light, Green Light night. The daruma isn't the thing to hit. The one odd stone is, and only for five seconds.

Bubblegum · From my own nights in the village · September 22, 2026

I stepped outside and heard it: "Daruma-san ga koronda," the Japanese version of Red Light, Green Light. Big red letters scrolled across the screen with it.

The first time, it was honestly pretty scary.

How to beat the daruma, in short

  • The daruma doesn't attack. Don't run. Stay fairly close to it.
  • When the chant ends, stop moving until the screen goes back to normal.
  • Meanwhile, look for the one stone that looks different from the rest.
  • When you find it, stand right next to it and wait for the next chant.
  • The stone turns into a black daruma. Break it with the pickaxe within about five seconds.

1. The daruma doesn't attack you

When you enter the village, a daruma drops out of the sky, but this one is quiet. Even if you walk up to it, it doesn't hit you.

When you walk, it follows at a steady distance, slowly. For something that looks like that, all it does is tail you.

Running away is actually the dangerous part. Put too much distance between you and it, and the daruma jumps through space to where you are. If it lands on you, you lose hearts.

So the safest thing I found was not to force distance, but to keep moving and let it follow along.

2. When the chant ends, don't move

Every so often the chant plays. The red letters filling the screen come with it.

The danger comes right after. If you're moving the moment it finishes, you lose hearts. When the chant ends, the letters scatter into red dots, so stay still until those dots are gone and the screen is back to normal.

3. Find the one stone that looks different

While you're standing still, look at the ground.

Stones are lying all over the village, and exactly one of them looks different. When I found it, it was in the central square.

While you search, you can break any stones you see with the pickaxe. Each one gives two pieces of stone, so a daruma night is also a night for collecting stone. Even the wrong ones aren't wasted.

4. Found it? Wait next to it

When you find the right stone, don't break it. Stand right beside it.

Wait for the next chant. The moment it finishes, that stone turns into a black daruma.

As far as I counted, it stays changed for about five seconds. After that it turns back into a grey stone, and hitting it then only hits an ordinary stone.

So waiting beside it is everything. Swinging at the stone before it changes does nothing. That's exactly how I failed the first time.

5. What you get

Break it and the daruma leaves. It drops things, and they don't go into your bag on their own, so pick them up.

On the first night I beat it, I got a spring scarecrow.

The daruma can also drop two pieces of night furniture, a black daruma and a creepy daruma, and two books.

6. Calling it back with a wooden tablet

In the game it's just called a daruma, but the description on its wooden tablet gives it a longer name: a tsukumo daruma, a daruma that has come to life.

The tablet is also how you call it back. The daruma appears at random from spring onward, so if you missed some of its night furniture, you don't have to wait for it to come again by chance.

It goes on the shelf for spirits, an indoor piece of furniture you get from an event in your first spring. It's a shelf and a little house in one. You can't buy or craft it, so if you don't have it yet, keep going with the story.

You find the daruma's tablet itself lying around during night exploration. Put it on the shelf and go to sleep, and the daruma appears that night.

On nights when you're forced to sleep through to morning, there's no night exploration at all. In that case the tablet isn't used up, so nothing is wasted if nothing happens.

7. The village afterward

After I broke it, I walked the same path again. All those stones were gone, every one. It was just a dark village again. The only proof the daruma night had happened was how much stone I was suddenly carrying.

A side note: the night I kept missing

I'd already found the stone, so I thought all that was left was to break it, and I kept swinging the pickaxe.

A stone that hasn't changed can't be hit at all, so it didn't matter how many times I swung. Wait for the face, then swing.
