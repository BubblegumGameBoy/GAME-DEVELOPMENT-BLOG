Village in the Shade: Fixing the Headless Japanese Doll (and What Happens After)

A headless doll behind the library. It turns in the night, then hops away. I followed it, and fixed it.

Bubblegum · From my own playthrough · September 28, 2026

A Japanese doll with its head missing, picked up in the village at night.

What would happen if I found its head and fixed it? I wanted to know, so I made one.

Finding the doll, in short

  • At night, pick up the headless doll behind the library and display it at home.
  • A few days later, on the night the doll disappears, go to the village entrance and follow the hopping doll to around the hunters' station.

1. Pick it up behind the library and display it

At night, behind the library, where the fire extinguisher box and the cardboard are, there's a Japanese doll with no head. You can only pick it up at night.

Take it home and display it anywhere. Each new day it'll be facing a different way, or lying knocked over. After a few days, you wake in the middle of the night and the doll is gone from the room.

2. On the night it's gone, follow the hopping doll

On the night it disappears, go out to the village. Around the village entrance, the doll is hopping along. Follow it. It stops around the hunters' station.

Inspect the spot where it stops and you get the doll's head and a blueprint for a Japanese doll. The headless doll comes back to you here too.

If you lose it, or morning comes before you notice, you can start over by picking up the doll behind the library again at night. You can pick it up as many times as you need until you finish.

Making the doll, in short

  • If you don't have a loom, make raw silk three times in a silkworm box and build a loom.
  • Put wool in the loom and make one piece of cloth.
  • At the workbench, combine the head, the body and the cloth.

1. No loom? Make raw silk first

You learn to make the loom at the shrine at the crossroads, for an offering of magatamas and a bronze mirror. It takes 15 wood and 3 raw silk, and the raw silk is the slow part.

Raw silk comes from putting mulberry leaves in a silkworm box. You learn the silkworm box at the shrine too, for another offering of magatamas and a bronze mirror. You build it at the workbench from 60 wood and 20 bamboo.

Mulberry leaves come from mulberry you plant in your field. The seedlings are sold at the shop for 2,000 yen, and ten days after planting you can pick leaves. They only grow leaves in summer, though.

Put the leaves in the silkworm box and two days later you have raw silk.

2. Weave wool into cloth

The cloth is made from wool. You can buy a lamb at the street stall for 2,500 yen. It grows up in ten days, and then gives wool every three days.

Put wool in the loom and in a day you have cloth. The doll needs one piece.

3. Make the doll at the workbench

At the workbench, combine the doll's head, the headless doll and one piece of cloth, and you have a Japanese doll.

So what happens once it's made?

With Japanese dolls, the classic story is the hair that grows at night. I expected something like that, the hair getting longer over time, or some special scene.

I've had it on display for a while, and so far nothing has happened. The head and body I picked up were both cursed items, but the fixed doll is night furniture, and unlike when it moved every night, it stays facing the way I put it.

The description changed too. When it was just the body, it seemed to have sad eyes. After fixing it, the description says it sometimes seems to smile.

Making the doll doesn't seem to lead to another event. And its hair, so far, hasn't grown.
