Village in the Shade Beginner Tips: 30 Things I Wish I'd Known in My First Spring

The game leaves a lot unexplained. Thirty things that would have made my first spring easier.

Bubblegum · After finishing the game in nearly fifty hours · October 11, 2026

Village in the Shade leaves a lot of its systems sitting there with no explanation. The water weeds you fish up are described as plain junk, and later they turn out to be a machine part. Nothing in the whistle's description tells you the dog can take you home.

I finished it in nearly fifty hours, so here's what would have made my first spring easier, one tip at a time, with screenshots.

Buttons are given for Switch. On PS5, read Y as Square, X as Triangle, L as L1, ZL as L2 and ZR as R2.

Your first spring

1. Clear the stumps and stones, and make a field

Your yard starts out full of stumps, stones and weeds. Trees and stumps take the axe, stones take the pickaxe. Till the cleared ground with the hoe and that's your field.

Hold X while walking to keep sowing seeds. Weeds in the field spread and hurt your crops if you leave them, so pull them when you see them.

2. Put storage boxes right next to the workbench

The first thing to make at the workbench is a wooden storage box. Your bag fills up fast, so this is where everything you pick up goes.

Put it next to the workbench. Storage boxes within the eight squares around the workbench can be used for crafting without taking things out. Once you have several boxes, name them with the − button so they're easy to find. If you put one in the wrong place, hold down on the D-pad to move it with everything still inside.

3. Make your first money fishing

Early on, fishing makes money faster than farming. At the village fishing spot in spring, satsukimasu trout sell well in the daytime and eels in the morning and at night. The occasional white eel sells for 2,500 yen on its own.

If nothing's biting, cast at different angles and spots rather than in the same place over and over. Once you learn to make groundbait at the shrine, fish bite more easily for a while after you throw it in.

4. Don't sell all your wild plants

Wild plants by the roadside restore stamina when you eat them, and sell for money. Don't sell them all, though. The fox shrine you meet at night asks for five each of butterbur sprouts, mitsuba and bracken in spring, and villagers ask for them too.

I keep one storage box just for wild plants, and anything I pick up goes there for now.

5. When the general store opens, buy a bag and a sickle

Once you can get into the general store, buy a bag and a sickle first. Each bag adds a row to your inventory. Mine has three rows now.

With only one row, you end up throwing things away out in the field before you head home. The sickle cuts weeds in a sweep, which makes keeping your field tidy and gathering materials much faster.

6. A yellow "!" on the map is the next part of the story

If there's a yellow "!" on the map, that's where the story continues. Some start just by going there. Others start when you talk to someone.

Open the map once at the start of each day and you'll hardly ever miss one.

Making daily chores faster

1. Hold Y while walking to water

Fill the watering can at the water behind your house. Holding Y while you walk is much faster than pressing it each time, and it waters everything you walk over.

On rainy days you don't need to water.

2. Hold L to see how many days until harvest

Hold L to focus on what's in front of you and see its details. For crops, days until harvest and their water and fertilizer state. For animals, how attached they are. For villagers, how much they like you and what they like and dislike.

If you lose track of what you've watered, a quick look tells you.

3. Whistle for the dog and it takes you home

On day 6 of spring, Komako teaches you to whistle with your fingers, and you can call the dog. To whistle, press ZR to bring up the tool wheel, then L. Play it as the notes show and the dog comes running.

Answer yes and it takes you home from anywhere in the village. On days you go deep into the mountains to fish or forage, you don't have to walk back. There are a few places you can't call it, though.

4. Pin what you want to make

Press ZL on the crafting or shop screen to pin an item. You can open the list with ZL while you're out, and see the money and materials you need all in one place. You don't have to remember what you're short of, so check it before heading to the quarry or the mountains and you'll waste fewer trips.

5. Stamina comes back with wild plants and food, hearts with boiled eggs

The hearts at the top left are your health, and the white bar under them is stamina. Stamina goes down with everything except walking, like tilling, chopping and fishing. When it's empty, you can only walk.

Food restores it. The number next to the flame icon in an item's description is how much stamina it gives back. Early on, eat wild plants. A little later, ration balls you can make at the workbench from wild seeds and roots. You can also pay to eat at the diner, and sleeping restores everything by morning.

Hearts go down when enemies in the quarry or spirits at night get you, and when they're gone, your day ends there. Dishes like boiled eggs bring them back.

6. Sleep before you switch off

There's no manual save. The game only saves when you sleep and wake up the next morning.

Switch off partway through the day and you start that day again from the morning. If you need to stop for a bit, put the Switch to sleep. When you're done for real, go to bed and let the morning come first.

Things to keep, not throw away

1. Weeds make a straw doll

The weeds you pull look useless. But the straw doll, which you learn to make at the shrine, needs 500 of them.

Just carrying a straw doll takes one hit for you, once. It's good insurance the first time you meet a spirit at night, so put your weeds in a storage box instead of throwing them away.

2. Water weeds you fish up make a composter

When you fish, sometimes you pull up empty cans, plastic bags or water weeds instead of fish. Keep the water weeds. The composter, which makes fertilizer, needs two.

The cans and bags have no use, so throw those away.

Villagers, animals and hunting

1. Talk to villagers every day

Just talking to villagers raises how much they like you. It only goes up once a day, so a quick hello as you pass is enough. When you focus on someone, a black speech-bubble icon means you haven't talked to them yet today.

Gifts raise it too, but you can only give two a week. Focusing shows what they like and dislike, so just don't give them anything they dislike. As they like you more, they give you recipes and invite you into their homes.

2. Give the village cats fish

There are cats in the village, and you can win them over. Walk up holding a fish to give it to them. Even cats that run when you get close will let you pet them once a fish raises their attachment one step.

After that, petting alone raises it, no fish needed. A cat you've won over completely starts visiting your house.

3. Board requests pay 1,000 yen each

You can take requests from the notice board from day 8 of spring. Give the person what they asked for before the deadline and you get 1,000 yen per request, and they like you more.

Deadlines are about a week, so you can fit them in between farming and fishing. Talking to them isn't enough. Choose the item from your bag to deliver it. Besides the board, villagers sometimes ask you directly while you're talking.

4. Achievement awards arrive in the mail

Repeat the same job enough times and it becomes an achievement. What counts are everyday jobs, like trapping animals or cutting grass. When you hit one, a letter congratulating your hard work arrives in your mailbox the next morning with a reward. It can be a good chunk of money, so check the mailbox often.

5. Buy chickens from Chinana at the stall

From day 10 of spring, you can keep chicks in a chicken coop. Animals are sold by Chinana at the street stall, which comes on Mondays, Wednesdays, Fridays and Sundays.

Two things every day: keep hay in the feed box, and pet them. Petting raises their attachment and the quality of the eggs. Chicks grow into hens in a few days and lay an egg every day. Boil them to restore hearts, or put them in the mayonnaise maker and sell them for more. Build a barn and you can keep cows and sheep too.

6. Put bait in your traps

There are two ways to hunt: shoot with a bow, or catch with a trap. Both need a hunting license for each animal, sold at the hunter's hut.

After you set a trap, don't forget the bait. Release a caught animal and you get a hunting certificate, which the hunters' station trades for meat and bones. The station is closed on Mondays and Tuesdays.

7. For deer, set the trap first and lure them in

Deer and boar charge at you when you aim your bow at them. For deer, it's easier to set a snare trap first, bait it, and wait nearby. The deer runs at you and straight into the trap.

Better tools and buildings

1. The quarry has a minecart every 10 floors

You can enter the quarry from day 8 of spring. Break rocks and you'll find holes down to the next floor. You don't have to break everything. Once you find the hole, go on down.

Every ten floors, a minecart unlocks, and next time you can ride straight down to it. Going ten floors at a time is fastest. Copper ore shows up on the shallow floors, iron ore from around floor 40. Touching enemies costs hearts, so take food that heals.

2. Smelt ingots in the furnace and upgrade your tools

Ore from the quarry becomes ingots in the furnace. Put coal or charcoal in with the ore and it's done in about twelve hours.

Ingots upgrade your farm tools at the anvil. Upgrade the watering can first. It waters a wider area, and your morning watering gets much shorter.

3. Unlock abilities at the shrine with magatamas

As the story goes on, you can learn abilities at the shrine at the crossroads. You pay in magatamas, which the general store sells and which you can also find at night.

Abilities are either new machines you can build, or effects that make farming and fishing easier. Every time you restore the shrine, the branches grow further.

4. Get sprinklers before anything else

The first shrine ability to go for is the sprinkler. A small sprinkler takes five copper ingots and five iron ingots, and waters the eight squares around it.

Set a few in your field and you stop walking around with the watering can. If you're short on magatamas, collect them on night walks and you'll get there sooner.

5. Ask Kisuke at the building company, one job at a time

For new sheds or a bigger house, you go to Kisuke at the building company. Give money plus wood or stone, and it's built in a few days.

You can only order one at a time, and only on weekday daytimes. Some take three days, so order as soon as you have the materials. The barn for cows and sheep is the one to build first. If fetching water is a long walk, a well makes watering easier too.

6. Plant a lot at once and quality rises faster

Harvest the same crop again and again and its quality rises, shown as stars. Stars go bronze, silver, gold. Bronze sells for 1.25 times the price, gold for 1.75 times.

If quality goes up partway through a harvest, the rest come in at the new quality. Planting a lot at once raises it faster than planting a few at a time, and earns more money.

7. Put sap tappers on different kinds of trees

The sap tapper you make at the workbench collects sap over time once you attach it to a fully grown tree.

Different trees give different sap, so put one on each kind of tree that looks different. Sap is a crafting material and villagers ask for it too.

Once you can go out at night

1. Use your flashlight to find dropped things

You pick up a flashlight on the night of spring day 14, and from around day 20 you can walk the village at night freely. What you're after at night are the things lying around that you can't find by day.

They're only visible in your flashlight's beam, so keep your light on as you walk. You'll find things like magatamas and mud seeds that the daytime doesn't give you. If you started in Village in the Sunshine mode, you can't go out at night.

2. Follow the fox, and make offerings at its shrine

If you see a fox in the village at night, follow it. It leads you to the fox shrine. In spring, the shrine asks for five each of butterbur sprouts, mitsuba and bracken.

Offer everything and you get fried tofu, which raises your max stamina. The shrine moves every season, and you can get fried tofu four times a year. More stamina means a lot more done in a day, which is one more reason to keep those wild plants. I put the shrine's locations and how to raise your hearts in a separate guide.

The village also has spirits at night. All my night guides are in the Village in the Shade section.
