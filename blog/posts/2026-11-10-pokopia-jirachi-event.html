Pokopia Jirachi Event Guide: Fireworks That Change Shape, and a Habitat That Needs Power

A limited-time event that ran in summer 2026. How it works, and six things you only find out by using the rewards.

Bubblegum · Played through the 2026 event · July 3, 2026

I launched a blue firework over my town at night, and it didn't open into a circle. It opened into a star.

That was during Wish Upon a Jirachi, Pokopia's Tanabata-season event. It ran from June 23 to July 8, 2026, and I started late. It had been going for days before I got serious, rushed to collect wish notes, and then got completely absorbed in the furniture I traded them for. Limited-time events like this have a habit of coming back, so this is everything I'd want to know the next time it runs.

The basics

  • To take part: at least one Pokémon Center rebuilt by finishing a town's important requests, the game updated, and an internet connection.
  • It doesn't happen on Cloud Islands.
  • Your Switch 2's date and time need to match real time.

It's a starry-sky event timed for Tanabata, the Japanese star festival where people write wishes on strips of paper. Jirachi, the wish-granting Pokémon, turns up outside your town's Pokémon Center. The first time I went to see it, my Jirachi looked half asleep. Adorable.

Collecting the sparkling wish notes

It's simple. Befriend Jirachi and you can craft wish notes at the crafting bench. The recipe is two vine ropes. You get vine rope by cutting the vines on mountain and cave walls with Cut, or by picking up what Grass-type Pokémon leave lying around.

Walk around town holding a wish note and you'll find Pokémon with a worried speech bubble over their heads. Talk to them, hear their wish and make it come true, and the wish note turns into a sparkling wish note.

Trade the sparkling wish notes at the Pokémon Center counter for star-themed furniture and more. There are ten rewards, each costing one sparkling note, so you need ten to get everything. That's twenty vine ropes in materials.

Six things I found by actually using the rewards

Here's the part you can't learn from the reward list.

1. The cloud launcher changes shape with the firework's color

Back to the blue firework. The event's cloud launcher isn't just decoration. A normal launcher fires a round firework whatever color you load. The event one opens in a different shape for each color.

I waited until night and fired every color, one at a time.

ColorShape
BlueA star. This was the one that surprised me most.
RedA Poké Ball, shape and all.
WhiteThe kind that trails down and hangs in the air, like at a big fireworks show.
GreenOpens in two stages, glowing green and yellow.
YellowJirachi's face. Unfairly cute.

It isn't random. The same color always gives the same shape, so you can pick the one you want. Greeting friends with Jirachi fireworks when they visit is now an option. Fireworks look far better at night, so test them after dark.

2. Eat food requests together, and you lose nothing

Some wishes are for a particular dish. At first I just cooked it and handed it over, which of course means the dish is gone. Fine for something simple, but a bit painful when they want something made with rare ingredients.

So I tried spending time with the Pokémon that asked, cooking right there and eating together. I got to eat the same dish, and it still counted as sharing, so the wish was granted. One round of cooking, and a portion for me too. I know it's a bit cheeky, but it helps most when someone asks for a rare dish.

3. Every reward except the launcher can change color

Looking at the furniture I'd traded for at the crafting bench, I noticed the event furniture has color variations. The bed and the closet change the whole mood of a room just by changing the star colors, and honestly I lost about an hour to it.

The one exception is the launcher from tip 1, which can't change color. I first wanted several of each piece in different colors, but since you can recolor afterward, one of each is enough. No need to collect extra notes.

4. The telescope actually works

I assumed the telescope was just for show. Place it and inspect it, though, and it zooms in so you can look at things far away. If your town is built up high, you can make a lookout over it. Pokopia is full of things like this that turn out to work.

5. Jirachi can have a habitat, but it needs electricity

I was building a room out of event furniture when a habitat suddenly registered. You can make one for Jirachi.

Mine registered with four pieces: the closet, the bed, the cloud table and the Jirachi lamp. A Jirachi living in a room surrounded by stars is really the goal of the whole event.

This is where I got stuck. With all four pieces in place, it still won't count as a habitat unless the Jirachi lamp has power. I forgot to wire it at first and spent a while wondering why nothing happened. If yours won't register, check the electricity first.

6. Jirachi leaves Stardust around

For me, this has the most practical value. Jirachi has the Litter specialty, but what it leaves lying around is special: Stardust.

Until then, Stardust was something you could only pick up on Dream Islands. Ninety-nine of it crafts one Star Piece, and you give Star Pieces to friends' Pokémon who visit your town over Link Play, in exchange for souvenirs. In other words, it's an important material for bringing friends' Pokémon to your town, and with Jirachi living in town it just piles up on its own.

If your Pokédex is already full, you may not need it right now. But new Pokémon arrived with the expansion, and Star Pieces may well matter again. Having done plenty of Dream Island runs, I think saving it up costs you nothing.

Bonus: trade for eight items and you get a frame

One more thing. When I made my eighth trade at the Pokémon Center, Jirachi started talking and gave me a photo frame, Shooting Star. It's a bonus on top of the ten rewards.

Even if you don't need much furniture, it's worth trading for eight if you take a lot of photos. And Jirachi says something every time you trade, so if you can, trade for all ten and hear it out.

When it comes back

I rushed through it to make up for starting late, and I'm glad I didn't just collect the rewards and stop there. Fireworks that change shape with color, a habitat that needs power, a Jirachi that leaves Stardust: none of it shows until you try the rewards.

Everything you need is twenty vine ropes, so even a late start gets you all of it. If you only pick one reward, I'd pick the launcher. Wait for night, and fire a blue one.
