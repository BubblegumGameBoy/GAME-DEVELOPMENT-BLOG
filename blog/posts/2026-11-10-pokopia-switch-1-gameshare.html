Pokopia on Switch 1: GameShare Works, but Only as the Guest

I ran Pokopia on a Switch 2 and an original Switch side by side. Here is how to connect them, what the second player can do, and where it fell apart.

Bubblegum · Tested with both consoles on the same table · April 12, 2026

On the Switch 2, my town looked the way it always does: sharp, bright, the second player's character walking right next to mine.

Next to it I put my old Switch, showing the same town at the same moment, and it looked noticeably worse. Lower resolution, rough edges, the kind of picture you notice from across the table.

"One copy of Pokopia and an old Switch is enough to play together" is true. Once I actually connected the two, though, a lot turned out to be missing from that one line.

The short answer

An original Switch can't run Pokopia on its own. The game is Switch 2 only. What the old Switch can do is join as a guest, through GameShare, while a Switch 2 hosts.

QuestionAnswer

With one Switch 2 in the house?Yes. The old Switch joins as the guest.

Where can you play?Palette Town only. You can't play the story together.
How many players?Two.
After the session?The guest can no longer play. Nothing carries over to the old Switch.

That's the spec. The rest of this page is what it was like to actually do it.

Connecting the two

On the Switch 2 (the one with the game)

GameShare only starts from the PC in Palette Town, so travel there first.

  1. Go to Palette Town.
  2. Use the PC in its Pokémon Center.
  3. Choose Link Play, then Invite Others to Visit, then GameShare.
  4. Pick the nearby, local option.

At step 4 you choose between local wireless and GameChat. For an old Switch it has to be local. GameChat needs the other player on a Switch 2 as well, plus a Nintendo Switch Online membership.

On the original Switch (the guest)

  1. On the HOME Menu, open GameShare.
  2. Choose Join GameShare.
  3. Wait for the Switch 2 to accept and start.

Up to here it all went smoothly.

Where it fell apart

Three things stacked up: the picture, the lag, and the constant stopping.

The picture is clearly worse on the old Switch

It's the scene from the top of this page. The Switch 2 looks great and the old Switch alone drops in resolution, and with the two side by side you see the difference every second.

If the guest is a child, I think this is where they get let down first. Same game, same town, and their screen is the rough one.

The lag comes often

It didn't feel like the game was struggling. It felt like the delay of streaming the game over local wireless. You aren't moving together smoothly so much as managing to stay in the same place.

Every time you run out, the session ends

This was the worst of it. During GameShare you can't leave Palette Town, so when materials run out nobody can go and get more. Whatever the host carried in is all there is.

When it runs out, the host ends the session, leaves town, gathers materials and starts again. Player two waits through all of that. A real share of the time we were supposedly playing together went to this.

Moves work, so watering, carrying and building are all fine. Asking someone to help you build the town is a real way to play.

Not being able to jump or glide is the part I think kids will mind. Those are the two things that feel best to do in this game, and having exactly those switched off is an obvious difference from the guest's side.

Nothing the guest collects stays with them. Their items go to the Lost & Found box when the session ends, and that box is in the host's town. Nothing builds up on the old Switch.

Should you set this up?

It's worth doing if:

  • you already have a Switch 2 and an old Switch at home
  • you're happy for the story to stay separate and only want to build together
  • sitting next to each other once, to try it, is enough

You will probably regret it if:

  • you're thinking of buying an old Switch just for this
  • you want to play the story together (you can't leave Palette Town)
  • the other player is a kid who will want to jump and glide
  • you notice blurry pictures and lag

If you want to play together properly, two Switch 2 consoles and two copies of the game is the reliable route. It costs a lot more, and you get the whole game rather than one town. GameShare lets someone try the game alongside you, and that's as far as it goes.

Still, it was nice to sit side by side

I've spent most of this page complaining, but it isn't all bad. Player two's character is cute. Seeing someone else walking around my town made me happy in a simple way, and while they were helping me build, it did feel like playing together. One copy of the game got the older console in the house back into use, and that alone makes it worth one try.

Even so, the two screens from the start stayed on my mind until the end. One of them was rough the whole time, and the feeling I had when I first saw that summed up the feature pretty well. You can share the game, but not the full experience of playing it.
