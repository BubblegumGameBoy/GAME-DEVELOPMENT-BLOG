Village in the Shade: The Tengu Candle Seller (Buy Both the Red and the Blue)

It switches off your light and then sells you candles. You can't beat it, but the candles show you things you'd miss.

Bubblegum · From my own nights in the village · September 22, 2026

Late at night, a letter arrived in my mailbox. No sender, no address. It announced that for tonight only, someone would be selling light, just for me.

After midnight, there's a door-to-door salesman for light in the village.

I read it and went out. My flashlight flickered a few times and went dark. I kept walking in the dark, and there was a tengu. I attacked it, and it didn't go down.

It's the one who sent the letter. It switched off your light itself, and now it's here to sell you some. No demand? Make some. A tengu with a real head for business.

You can't beat this tengu. Attacks don't work, and there's nothing to break like the lanterns on other nights. It sells candles until morning and leaves when it gets light.

Your flashlight isn't broken either. It just stays off while the tengu is around. You buy your light from the tengu.

What to do, in short

  • Late at night, a letter arrives. It's for tonight only, so go to the village that night.
  • Buy a candle from the tengu at the shopfront. 500 mon each.
  • Carry it, and dropped items of the same color become visible.
  • Red and blue candles show different drops. If both tengu are there, buy both.
  • Use the shards you pick up to make night furniture, like wall masks.

1. It starts with a letter

A tengu night begins with a letter in your mailbox. It's the second line that's easy to skim past: tonight only. If you don't go that night, the shop is gone. Tengu come at random, so you don't know when the next one will be.

When the letter comes, don't go to sleep. Head straight out to the village.

2. The red tengu is at a shopfront

The tengu sets up under a shop's eaves. The one I met was in front of the general store.

You won't have trouble finding it. It has dozens of candles lit, and that one spot glows red in the dark village, so you can see it from far away once you reach the center. With your own light out, it stands out even more.

A candle costs 500 mon. The game shows the old "mon" coin rather than the usual money, but it really did take 500 from me.

500 isn't nothing early on, and you may hesitate. My answer: buy it. Here's why.

3. With a candle, hidden drops appear

Light the candle and walk, and dropped items of the same color appear on ground that was empty before. Pick them up and they become red shards. Not every time, though. Sometimes there's nothing.

Without a candle you can't see these drops at all. They only appear on tengu nights, so if you get through the night without buying one, everything that was lying around that night is gone. Think of the 500 as the entry fee.

4. Collect shards and make wall masks

The shards you collect become night furniture through crafting. The blueprints are among the drops too.

Night furnitureShardsOther materials
Wall-mounted tengu mask15 red2 kinds, 1 each
Wall-mounted hannya mask15 blue2 kinds, 1 each

You need 15 shards. I had 16 and still couldn't make it, because you also need two other materials, one of each.

This is where color matters. The blueprint I picked up was for the hannya mask, which only takes blue shards. Blueprints drop at random, so if you only collect one color, you end up holding blueprints for masks you can't make.

5. There's a blue tengu too. Buy from both.

Besides the red tengu, there's a blue one. It sells blue candles at the same 500 mon. Carry one and blue drops appear, and you pick them up as blue shards. From there to night furniture, it's exactly the same as red.

If both are out, buy both. One candle only shows its own color, so save 500 and you'll walk right past half of what's lying on the ground that night. And since blueprints don't pick a color for you, you'll end up needing both anyway.

6. To call it again, use the tengu's wooden tablet

Tengu come at random, so waiting for one can take a while. If you're short on shards, you can call it.

Put it on the spirit shelf. The game calls this an offering rather than placing it.

The tablet is used up when it works. Put it there before the night you want and go to sleep.

There are wooden tablets for other spirits too, and each description names the spirit it calls. If you meet one whose name you don't know, its tablet tells you what it's properly called.

On a tengu night you'll walk the village end to end many times. With few hearts and little stamina, you'll often go home before you've picked everything up. Both can be raised, and it makes the night last longer.
