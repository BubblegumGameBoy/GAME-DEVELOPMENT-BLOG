The expensive units in Mythos Slayer are a trap, and I am the one who built them

I made this game. I also had to sit down and work out how to play it properly, which is a slightly embarrassing thing to admit.

Here is the situation I kept getting into with my own game.

Gold piles up. A unit I have never been able to afford finally goes from grey to lit. I buy it immediately, because that is what the whole loop has been training me to do for the last twenty minutes. And then progress gets slower, and I sit there wondering whether I broke something.

I did not break anything. I bought the wrong thing, in a game I wrote.

What a unit actually costs you

The number the game shows you is the price. The number that matters is the price divided by the damage per second you get for it.

So I pulled every unit out of the game data and did that division. This is the result, and it is worse than I expected.

The Swordsman buys a point of damage per second for 40 gold. The Demon God buys the same point for 500,000.

The most expensive unit in the game is roughly 12,500 times less gold-efficient than the cheapest one. Every step up that ladder is a worse deal than the step before it, and the game never says so. It just lights the button up and waits.

Two units break the pattern, and they are the best purchases in the game

Look again at the Ancient Jade Dragon and the Awakened Crimson Dragon. Their gold-per-DPS goes down compared to what sits either side of them, which happens nowhere else in the list.

The reason is a single property in their data: they fire three projectiles instead of one. That triples their real output while the price only follows the normal curve.

The Archangel just above them costs 320,000 per DPS. The Ancient Jade Dragon costs 150,000. It is less than half the price for the same damage, and it is the next unit up.

I would like to claim that was a considered piece of design. Honestly it started as a way to make the big dragons feel appropriately dramatic on screen, and the balance implication only became obvious later, when I did this exact division.

So how should you actually play

The efficiency table looks like it says "only ever buy Swordsmen", and that is wrong, because damage is not the only thing being bought.

Boss HP follows 300 × 2.42^(wave-1). It multiplies by 2.42 every single step, forever. Cheap units cannot keep pace with that no matter how many you stack, because you run out of places to put them long before the maths works out.

Which gives you the actual rule:

  • Early on, do not skip tiers. The cheap units are so much more efficient that buying up too fast is the most common way to stall out. Keep buying what you can afford in volume
  • Buy the next tier when you are stuck, not when you can afford it. Affordability is the trap. Being unable to progress is the actual signal
  • Prioritise the Ancient Jade Dragon and the Awakened Crimson Dragon. They are the only two purchases in the game that get cheaper per point of damage. Reaching them is worth planning around
  • Do not neglect the Upgrade tab. Kitchen Knife costs 60 gold and carries the entire opening before you own a single unit. Crit chance and crit damage keep scaling your tapping long after your tapping should have stopped mattering

Rebirth is arithmetic, not vibes

Rebirth unlocks at Boss 10 and resets everything for a permanent gold multiplier. The formula is:

multiplier = 1 + 0.4 × deepest boss cleared

Reset after clearingPermanent gold multiplier
Boss 10 (earliest possible)x5
Boss 15x7
Boss 20x9
Boss 28x12.2
Boss 40x17

Ten extra bosses before you reset is worth four extra permanent multipliers. That is a lot to give up for the satisfaction of pressing the button the moment it unlocks.

An earlier version of the game did not work this way. Resetting instantly was always correct, so there was no decision in it at all, and I rebuilt the formula specifically to put one back.

The practical advice: do not reset at Boss 10 unless you are properly stuck. Push until the wall is real, then take the bigger multiplier.

The part I got wrong for a while

For a long stretch the version of this guide that existed said the opposite. It told you to prestige early, and that the multiplier was a flat permanent doubling.

That was written from memory of an older build and it was simply wrong. The bosses were listed wrong too, and so was the unit count. Nobody ever pointed any of this out to me. I found it by opening my own game files to write this rewrite, which is a strange way to receive a bug report.

Anyway. The numbers above are the ones in the shipping build. If they change, it is because I changed them, and I will come back and fix this page again.
