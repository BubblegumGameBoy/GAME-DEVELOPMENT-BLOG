Village in the Shade: The Straw Hat Ghosts and the Jizo Statues (How Not to Run Out of Time)

On rainy nights, one-legged hats chase you. You don't fight them. You catch four and give them to the jizo.

Bubblegum · From my own rainy nights in the village · September 23, 2026

Walk the village on a rainy night and a one-legged straw hat comes after you.

You don't beat this one. You catch it and put it on a jizo statue. Four of them.

The task itself is simple, but it's very easy to run out of time. The night I finally did it, I finished at 2:30 in the morning with one and a half hearts left.

How to deal with the hat ghosts, in short

  • They only come out on rainy nights. When they do, go straight to the village.
  • The row of jizo statues is down and to the right of the clock tower. Learn where it is first.
  • Sidestep the hat's charge. When it stops, pick it up.
  • At a jizo, choose yes when asked whether to put the hat on. Do this four times.
  • A picked-up hat turns back into a ghost after a while, so do your dodging right next to the statues.

1. Rainy night: go straight to the village

The hat ghosts only appear on rainy nights.

That night, four hats find you, and you take them to the statues one by one. Find, dodge, carry, four times over. If you're slow getting to the village, that alone can make you miss the 5 a.m. limit. On a rainy night, leave everything else for later and head straight in.

On rainy days, if you put another spirit's wooden tablet on the spirit shelf, the hat ghosts won't come. Rainy days are rare, so leave the shelf empty on days you want them. If you run out of time, a soft reset before you go home lets you redo the night from the start.

2. The jizo statues are down and to the right of the clock tower

There are four jizo in a row with a stone wall behind them. The hat ghosts come from all over the village, so learning where the goal is first keeps you from getting lost.

3. Dodge sideways, then pick it up

Get close to a hat ghost and it winds up, then charges in a straight line. If it hits you, you lose hearts.

It only comes straight, so step sideways to its line and it misses. A dodged hat stops where it is and becomes a plain straw hat. Pick it up and carry it.

You'll sometimes read that you need to make it hit a wall. You don't. Dodging is enough to stop it.

I've been calling it a hat ghost, but most people know this kind of yokai as the kasa-obake, the umbrella ghost. Pick it up, though, and it turns out to be a woven straw hat, not an umbrella.

Watch the ground too. On rainy nights there are pools of blood on the paths. Step in one and white hands come out and hold you in place for a while. Dodging a charge straight into a blood pool is the worst thing that can happen, so look at your feet when you sidestep.

4. Choose yes at the jizo

Walk up to a jizo while carrying a hat and talk to it, and it asks if you want to put the hat on.

There's no real reason to say no. Put the hat on and go catch the next one.

With all four covered, the row looks like the picture at the top of this page.

5. Dodge right next to the statues

A straw hat you've picked up turns back into a ghost after a while. If you dodge and grab it far from the statues, it can change back while you're carrying it, and you're back to the chase.

What I did was walk around the village letting several hats spot me, then lead them all to the statues together. Hats that have found you follow you, so you can pull them close. The carrying distance gets shorter and it works more often, but you're chased by several hats at once.

To go one step further, dodge each one right beside the statues. The spot where you dodge is already next to a jizo, so you can pick it up and put it on immediately, with almost no carrying.

From dodge to hat-on-statue took me about eight seconds. You're still chased by several at once, but each one becomes dodge, pick up, put on, so they shouldn't turn back into ghosts while you carry them.

6. After all four

Put the fourth hat on and a short scene plays. At the end, a hat ghost lunges at you one last time.

Then it's gone, and it leaves something behind.

7. What you get

A hat-ghost night can give you two pieces of night furniture, wall-hanging straw hats in two colors, and rarely a straw hat you can wear, which you then put on at the dresser or wardrobe.

On my first time, what it left was a summer scarecrow.

I got it on a spring night, and it was a summer scarecrow. According to its description, set it in a field and for 28 days, summer crops and flowers grow in the 5×5 squares around it, so you can start summer vegetables while it's still spring.

The hats chased me all night, and what they left me was a scarecrow to stand in my field.
