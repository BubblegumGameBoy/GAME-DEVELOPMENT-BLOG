Long words summon dragons: how Cthulhu Typing rewards you for not panicking

I made this game. The numbers below come out of my own source rather than from playing and guessing.

In my first typing game, keystrokes were attacks. Type fast, deal damage. It works, and it has one problem I could not stop noticing: if you are slow, you lose. Not eventually, not narrowly. You just do not get to finish.

So in this one, keystrokes do not attack. They summon.

Your words put soldiers on the field, and the soldiers do the fighting on their own timer, every 1.2 seconds, whether or not you are still typing. A slow typist builds an army more slowly than a fast one, but the army does not care how it got there. Given enough time, a slow player still wins.

The word length is the whole game

What you type does not just decide how many soldiers appear. It decides which soldiers appear.

Word lengthTierWho shows upDamage
1 to 2ApprenticeVillager, Farmer6
3WarriorSwordsman, Dwarf Warrior, Dwarf Soldier9
4EliteKnight, Heavy Infantry, Archer, Elf Archer12 to 13
5HeroWizard, Warlock, and above18+

Five characters is the longest word in the pool, which makes it the top tier by definition. There is a comment in my source that says exactly that: the word list maxes out at five, so five is where the dragons and angels live.

A five-letter word is worth three times a two-letter word, per soldier. Not three times the letters. Three times the damage, permanently, from a unit that then keeps attacking for the rest of the fight.

Which produces the one piece of advice that actually matters here: when a long word comes up and you are not sure of it, take the extra half second and get it right. Rushing a five-letter word into a mistake trades a Warlock for nothing. Nailing it slowly still buys you a Warlock.

There are 465 unique words in the pool, which is the largest word list I have written for anything.

Everyone fights the same boss on the same day

There are 43 bosses. You do not choose between them and you cannot grind them. The game picks one:

todaysBoss = ROSTER[dayIndexInJST % 43]

The date is calculated in Japan time, so the boss rolls over at midnight JST regardless of where you are. Everyone playing on a given day is fighting the same thing, which is the entire reason the leaderboard is interesting rather than just a list of who has played most.

One run per day. That constraint was the point of building it.

Boss HP, and the fairness problem it created

Boss HP is linear, not exponential:

hp = 16,000 + index × 520

So the weakest boss in the rotation has 16,000 HP and the strongest has 37,840. That is a spread of more than double.

Which broke the leaderboard the moment I thought about it. If today's boss has 16,000 HP and tomorrow's has 37,840, then tomorrow's scores are inflated by nothing more than the calendar. Being good would matter less than showing up on a Thursday.

The fix is one line:

strength = todaysBossHP / 24,000

Every score is divided by that. 24,000 is the reference point where the multiplier is exactly 1.0, and everything else scales around it. A monster day gets its scores deflated, a soft day gets them inflated, and what survives is how well you actually typed.

It is a small piece of arithmetic that took an embarrassingly long time to think of, and without it the ranking would have been meaningless.

There is no limit on your army

The sprite cap in this game is 2,000, which the source comment describes as effectively unlimited. I have never come close to it.

That is a deliberate contrast with my clicker, where the number of units you can field is bounded by screen space and that limit shapes the entire late game. Here, the field just keeps filling up. If you type well for long enough, the boss disappears behind your own soldiers.

Watching that happen is most of why I built it.

What I would tell you if you asked

  • Accuracy over speed on long words. A five-letter word is a Hero-tier unit. Do not throw it away going fast
  • Short words are not wasted. A Villager at 6 damage still attacks every 1.2 seconds forever. Volume is real
  • Do not panic at the strong bosses. The score already accounts for them. A good run against a 37,840 HP boss scores the same as a good run against a 16,000 one
  • Come back tomorrow. That is the whole shape of the game. One boss, one day, one attempt

Nobody has ever asked me any of this, in fairness. There are no comments on this game and no messages about it. I wrote it down because I opened the source to check something else and realised the score normalisation is invisible to anyone playing, and it is the part I am proudest of.

Cthulhu Typing is free in the browser. It is Japanese typing practice, typed in romaji.
