Piano Typing · Behind the game

Typing practice is boring and piano practice is not, and the only difference is sound

Bubblegum · June 26, 2026 · updated August 11, 2026

I made this game. This is why it exists and what nearly killed it.

Sit with this for a second, because it's the entire reason this game exists.

Typing practice is repeating the same finger movements until they stop needing thought. Piano practice is repeating the same finger movements until they stop needing thought. Structurally they are the same activity.

People quit typing practice in a week. People do piano for decades.

The difference isn't discipline. It's that one of them makes a sound when you get it right, and the other one moves a counter.

So I made the drill into an instrument

Piano Typing is home-row practice. Keys light up, you press them, and what comes out is an actual piece of music. There are three of them:

PieceAlso known as
My Grandfather's ClockThe one everyone in Japan learns as a child
Für EliseBeethoven. You know it even if you think you don't
Turkish MarchMozart. The fast one

The important design decision, and the one I would defend hardest: the game doesn't play the song for you. It would have been far easier to trigger a backing track and let your keystrokes fire off decorative sound effects on top. Plenty of rhythm games do exactly that and they feel fine.

I didn't want the illusion of playing. I wanted the thing itself. Every note you hear is a key you pressed. Stop pressing and the music stops, in the middle of a phrase, sounding exactly as wrong as it would on a real piano.

The tradeoff is that a bad run sounds bad. Which is correct. That's what practising is.

Almost all of the work was in the delay

Here is where the project nearly fell over.

The gap between pressing a key and hearing the note has to be effectively zero. Not "fast". Not "under a tenth of a second, that's fine". If there's any perceptible delay at all, the feeling of playing evaporates instantly and completely, and what you're left with is a typing drill with sound effects attached, which is the thing I was trying to escape.

It's a strange failure mode to debug, because nothing is broken. Nothing errors. The game just doesn't feel like anything. You press a key, a sound occurs, and your hands and your ears have quietly concluded they are two separate events.

Browsers don't make this easy. The audio stack has its own scheduling, and the naive approach of creating a sound when a key goes down puts you at the mercy of it. I ended up on Tone.js for the timing control, and the majority of my hours on this project went into that one problem rather than into anything you could point at on screen.

When it finally landed, the difference wasn't subtle. Same notes, same graphics, same everything, and suddenly your hands believe they are doing it.

The sneaky part

The key assignment places the most-used notes on the home row before using other letter keys. Playing them is finger placement practice, and you aren't thinking about finger placement at all, because you're thinking about Für Elise.

That's the whole trick, and it isn't a very complicated one. Give the drill a reason to exist that isn't the drill.

Would I do it again

Three songs isn't many, and I know it. The reason there are three rather than thirty is that each one has to be arranged to fit the home row and then tested for feel, and the second part takes much longer than the first.

Nobody has ever asked me for a fourth song. Nobody has asked me anything about this game at all. But it's the one I go back and open for no reason, which is probably the most honest review I can give it.

Piano Typing is free in the browser. Headphones recommended, obviously.
