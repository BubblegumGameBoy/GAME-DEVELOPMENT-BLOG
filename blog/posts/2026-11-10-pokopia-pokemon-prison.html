I Built a Pokémon Prison in Pokopia: Pens, Birdcages and a Jail in the Sky

A Pokémon inside a fenced pen can't move. That one rule took my Cloud Island somewhere I don't think the developers had in mind.

Bubblegum · Built over about ten hours on my Cloud Island · November 10, 2026

Next to my house on the Cloud Island there's a hot spring, and it looks lovely. Right beside it, in a small fenced pen, there's a Squirtle.

The Squirtle doesn't run away. It can't.

Most people would never play Pokopia like this, and I doubt the developers expected anyone to. Here's how it happened.

The two rules that make it possible

Every Pokémon in Pokopia has things it's good at, like supplying electricity or watering plants. And when you place a Pokémon inside a fenced pen, it can no longer move out of it.

Put those together and you can probably see where this is going.

The hot spring, and the Squirtle behind it

I built the hot spring next to my house, and on the surface it's a nice one. Then there's the pen next to it with the Squirtle inside. A Water-type placed near water looks like it's supplying the water. The hot spring doesn't actually need any, but it really does look as if the Squirtle has been put to work.

This one is genuinely useful. Lamps and other equipment need a Pokémon's electricity to run. Pen an Electric-type like Raikou or Ampharos behind a lamp and it keeps the power going on its own. I left it like that for more than ten hours and it never got tired. It's still working.

Too many Pokémon, so they all went in the basement

As the island grew and more Pokémon moved in, I kept losing track of them. Walking the whole island to find one got old fast.

My solution was to put everyone in one place, underground.

Under this house is a Pokémon prison, complete with security cameras. Everyone is kept here. Unpaid. You can see where every Pokémon is at a glance, which is extremely convenient.

Escapes, and the double birdcage

Keep Pokémon in pens for a while and you notice something. Some of them slip through. You put them to work, and the next time you look, they're gone.

So I built a double birdcage. The inner pen holds the Pokémon as usual, and a larger pen goes around the outside. If one gets out of the inner pen, the outer one stops it.

Build the outer pen with as few gaps as you can, and use tall walls so Pokémon have a harder time getting over. Put it together with the basement and you have holding, watching and escape prevention all in one place. I'm sorry, Pokémon. It's operational necessity.

Then I built a jail in the sky

The basement wasn't enough, so I built a prison up in the sky too. I call it the Azkaban of Pokopia.

For the game to count a cell as a room, it needs bars and a door together. I assumed that with nothing below the door but a long fall, nobody would leave. They kept disappearing anyway. I watched to find out why, and the answer was that they were jumping. Pokémon were throwing themselves off the edge rather than stay.

So I sealed the doors so no one could jump, and Azkaban was complete.

Strange things happen in prison

A Chansey was keeping its own egg warm. Think about that for a second and it starts heading somewhere dark. Maybe prison got to it.

Next, an experiment: pushing a Snorlax off the edge. It's heavy, so I wanted to see if it would fall faster. Halfway down it froze in mid-air, a bug of some kind. After that it sank deep into the water and I couldn't get it back. As far as the game is concerned it still exists. I just can't see it anywhere. All I know is that it's somewhere under the water.

Plusle and Minun, or rather two fakes. I still haven't found the real ones. Do they show up at all?

Ethics aside, the fact that "fence them in and they'll work automatically" actually works makes me think the developers may have allowed for it. Or maybe this is just normal in this world.

Can you beat up your friends?

About a minute after joining, I tried attacking a friend. There's no HP, so nobody gets knocked out. You can, however, box someone in so they can't move. Pokémon moves are tools for building, not for hurting other players. It's a peaceful world, mostly.

One friend had put down an enormous minecart track and built a base right up at the height limit.

I punched them, and they fell all the way to the bottom.

Visit it yourself

You can look around my island in Virtual Mode with the address TX699DP0. No password needed. The basement prison and Azkaban are both there. If you don't know how visiting works, my Cloud Island guide explains it, along with nine other islands worth seeing.

And the Snorlax, as far as I know, is still asleep at the bottom of the sea. In a way, it's the only one that got out.
