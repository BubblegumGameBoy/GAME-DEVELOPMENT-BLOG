I Built a Pokémon Zoo in Pokopia: Glass Tanks, Habitats and a Gyarados

After the prison, something that actually thinks about the Pokémon. Each enclosure is built around where that Pokémon would live.

Bubblegum · Still being built on my Cloud Island · April 6, 2026

Last time, I built a Pokémon prison. This time I wanted to build something that actually considers the Pokémon.

I've always liked reptiles and miniature landscapes, and keeping animals in tanks. So I wondered what that would look like with Pokémon, and decided to build a zoo.

The plan

The idea is simple: build an environment that suits each Pokémon and let it live there. Not just a fence, but a little landscape built around the right biome.

From the prison, I already knew that a Pokémon placed inside an enclosure can't move out of it. Used zoo-style, that should make a proper exhibit. The difference between a zoo and a prison is whether the residents are comfortable. Probably.

It's still a work in progress, but this is the entrance. I put the Sudowoodo from the April Fools' event there, and somehow it fits. I really like that Sudowoodo. It also made me a lot more hopeful about what future updates will add.

That Sudowoodo is a limited item, so you can't make it on a Cloud Island the normal way. You can bring it over with the 3D printer, though.

The exhibits

Vaporeon, the amphibian zone

The first one I built was Vaporeon. I displayed a Water-type the way you'd show an amphibian, and it really does look like something being kept and cared for.

I wanted driftwood for a rainforest look, but there's no driftwood item, so I used potted trees instead. It works better than I expected.

Squirtle, the riverside zone

Next was Squirtle. The theme is a riverside, with a pool of muddy water at the front. It feels like a real miniature landscape, and I can look at it for ages.

Gyarados, the tank

Gyarados got a tank. Getting the game to count it as a home was hard. What worked was putting grass, pipes and other things in the water and making the habitat from the biome.

I hadn't known you could build fish tanks in Pokopia. Depending on how you use glass and other clear materials, you can make something that looks a lot like a real aquarium.

Cyndaquil, the volcano zone

Cyndaquil is on a volcano base. I want to collect all the starters, but I'm not there yet. I'd also like to build bigger landscapes, maybe a forest with something huge like Venusaur in it.

Peakychu, the electric zone

Peakychu, the pale Pikachu. I couldn't decide whether to give it something wild-looking, so for now it lives in a house. How to show an Electric-type's biome is something I still need to work out.

Building it with a kid in the family

Several people can build on a Cloud Island, so I'm working on this one with a young relative who's in elementary school. It's fun to come back and find buildings that weren't there before.

This Pikachu is theirs. They seem to build it like pixel art, block by block, which is not how I would ever have thought to do it.

I've heard that Lego and Minecraft get used in education. Playing this, I kept thinking Pokopia would be pretty good at that too.

On a Cloud Island, anyone with the address and password can get in at any hour, even when the host isn't online. Even if your schedules don't line up, each of you can build a bit whenever you have time.

Trying an automatic fruit collector

Separate from the zoo, a friend and I tried an automatic collector I'd seen in a video, a setup that gathers fruit on its own.

It's harder than it looks. I came away thinking it's more practical to just combine Pokémon by their specialties and let the items pile up. Still, figuring out a contraption like this is fun.

The basic version: put the habitats of a Pokémon with the Litter specialty and a Pokémon that sorts items close together, place a storage box nearby, and collection starts on its own. What you collect depends on the Pokémon you pair. My personal pick is Haxorus.

What's next

  • Finish two more starter zones, for Scorbunny's and Rowlet's lines
  • Work out how to show an Electric-type biome
  • Make the tank much bigger and build an ocean zone
  • Scale up the collector so it handles more than one material

The zoo is far from finished, and I'll keep adding to it.

You can visit in Virtual Mode with the Mysterious Goggles at TX699DP0. No password needed, and both the zoo and the prison are open. The Squirtle that used to be shut behind the hot spring now has a proper riverside of its own. Progress.
