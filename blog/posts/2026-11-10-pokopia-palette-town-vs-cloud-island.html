Palette Town vs Cloud Island in Pokopia: Which One to Use With Friends

Both are blank land you build from nothing, and I mixed them up. One needs the host online. The other doesn't, but you keep only recipes.

Bubblegum · Written from playing both with friends · July 27, 2026

I told a friend "I'll send you the address, hang on," and then stood in front of the PC for about five minutes.

The address wasn't anywhere.

Of course it wasn't. Only Cloud Islands have addresses, and I was standing in Palette Town. Both are blank pieces of land you build up from nothing, the names even sound a bit alike, and for a while I thought they were the same thing.

They're two different places to play. Mix them up when you're meeting friends and they'll never get in. I lost thirty minutes just getting everyone into the same place.

The difference

The biggest difference is whether the host has to be there. Palette Town lives inside the host's save, so nobody can get in unless the host has the game open. A Cloud Island lives outside your Switch, on the server. Even if the person who built it is asleep, anyone with the address can walk in.

The other difference you'll feel is where things end up. What you build in Palette Town stays with the host. On a Cloud Island, every material you picked up is collected by the PC when you leave. All the ore you dug out is gone, and the first day that stings a little.

Which one should you use?

If you can get together at the same time, Palette Town is enough. It's free, and the host can bring their own things in, so building goes fast. For spending half a weekend with a friend, it's the comfortable choice.

If you're adults whose online hours never line up, it has to be a Cloud Island. That's why I settled there. I level some ground alone late at night, and by the next morning a friend has carried on from where I stopped. It moves forward like a shared diary.

If you can't decide, go by whether you can meet at the same time. The two don't interfere with each other, so you can try free Palette Town first and move to a Cloud Island once you find your schedules don't match.

How to get to Palette Town

You'll often read that Palette Town is "past the road to the west," but the first time, the road seems to stop partway and you turn back. I did, once, convinced it was a dead end. Here's the route in pictures.

1. Go through the cave where Professor Tangrowth is

Carry on with the early tutorial and head toward the Pokémon Center.

2. Find the broken bridge to the left of the Pokémon Center

At the Pokémon Center, go round to the left. There's a bridge with a section missing. Head past it.

3. Keep going until you see something like an old highway

Walk on and a large road like the remains of a highway comes into view. You're going the right way.

4. Break the sand at the gap and connect the road

The road has collapsed partway, so you can't cross as it is. Break the sand blocks around it to make footing, and open the road yourself.

Palette Town is at the end of that road. When you finally get there it's almost anticlimactic: huge, and completely empty.

Playing with friends in Palette Town

Multiplayer opens when the Withered Wastelands reach Environment Level 2. That took me about an hour from a new game, so you can get there on the first evening.

  1. Go to Palette Town. It's the place for building together. Invite someone to another town and they can only look around, with no moves and no items.
  2. At the PC, choose Link Play, then Invite Others to Visit.
  3. Choose a connection and set a password. In the same room, pick the nearby option. Far apart, pick internet and tell them the password.
  4. Guests choose to visit a friend's town and enter the password. Up to 4 players. When the host quits, everyone is sent home, so it's polite to say before you leave.

Joining from an original Switch through GameShare only works over local wireless. Online GameShare is Switch 2 to Switch 2 only, so a friend far away on an original Switch can't join, sadly. I tried the local version, and here's how that went.

Making a Cloud Island (host)

  1. Reach Environment Level 2 and have Nintendo Switch Online. The unlock is the same as Palette Town, but a Cloud Island needs a membership.
  2. At the PC, go to Link Play and choose to play on a Cloud Island.
  3. Create a new island. Choose how the land is made: random, or a nine-digit seed.
  4. Name it and choose who can come. Open to everyone means anyone can walk in while you're away. Setting a password limits it to people who know it. For playing with people you know, that's the safer choice.
  5. Share the address and password. You can check both any time on the PC in the Cloud Island's own Pokémon Center. That's exactly what I was looking for at the start.

Joining a friend's Cloud Island (guest)

  1. From Link Play, choose to play on a Cloud Island and search for a new one.
  2. Enter the address and password from your friend. After the first time you can pick it from the islands you've visited before, so you don't need to remember it.

Things to know before you start

  • Up to 4 at once. When someone leaves, someone else can come in, so in practice there's no limit.
  • If it's open to everyone, people can visit at any hour, even with the host away.
  • Items and materials are collected by the PC when you leave. You can't take them home.
  • New recipes you learn stay with you, for both host and guests.
  • Pokémon you meet can't come home with you, and Pokédex entries only count on the island.
  • Changing the console clock doesn't work there, so build time tricks are out.
  • Limited-time events don't happen on Cloud Islands.
  • One Cloud Island at a time. To make a new one, you have to delete the current one.

Losing everything you gather is the first letdown, but it's also an opening. Materials disappear, yet recipes stay in your own save. The Cloud Island shop also has its own daily stock, separate from your story towns, so checking it whenever you log in picks up recipes quickly. These days I drop in to check the shop more often than to dig.

Before you delete an island to start over, note down its seed. Deleting it loses everything you built, but with the seed you can recreate the same land later.

Fixing the land with a seed

The nine-digit number you can enter when making an island is a seed. Enter the same number and you get the same land every time. Even if you let the game pick at random, you can find your island's seed in the settings on its PC.

Good or bad land doesn't matter that much in practice, but having the Pokémon Center near the middle makes getting around much easier.

Or just look around

The Mysterious Goggles, sold once you reach Environment Level 3, let you look around someone else's Cloud Island in Virtual Mode with just the address. No password, and no building. I wrote up how they work, with the official islands to try.

My own island is at TX699DP0, basement prison and sky jail included.

In short

If you can meet at the same time, use free Palette Town. If you can't, use a Cloud Island you can hand out an address for, with Nintendo Switch Online. Palette Town is west of the Withered Wastelands, at the end of the broken highway you fix yourself. On a Cloud Island, only recipes come home, so dig expecting the materials to disappear.

And when a friend asks for the address, go and stand at the PC on the Cloud Island, not the one in Palette Town.
